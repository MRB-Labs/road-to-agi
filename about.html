About

Road to AGI maps the physical infrastructure behind AI: ten layers, from the energy and materials at the bottom to the machines that act in the world at the top.

Who writes it

Mark Rühle Bosch, a senior mechanical engineer at Lumentum in Zürich, where he develops advanced photonics and laser systems. He holds an MSc in mechatronics engineering, and bachelor’s degrees in mechanical engineering and in electronics and automation engineering. He is a long-term investor and technology analyst, following AI infrastructure, semiconductors, hyperscalers and robotics.

Why it exists

It comes from an interest in where engineering, physics and economics meet, and in how large language models, AI agents and physical infrastructure come together to create real-world value. Its aim is to teach: to make visible what makes frontier technology possible.

How it is made

Facts are cited to named sources, estimates and derived calculations are labelled as such, and opinions are kept apart from both. Where a claim is time-sensitive it carries the date it was checked, or its layer's as-of line where it has not yet been re-verified. Sources are ranked before they are used — see the method and the source register. Every release is checked automatically before it is published, and what changed is recorded in the changelog.
